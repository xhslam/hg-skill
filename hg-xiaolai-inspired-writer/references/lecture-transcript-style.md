# Lecture Transcript Style Notes

Source set:
- `教练的真相之对象_原文.docx`
- `教练的真相之复盘_原文.docx`
- `教练的真相之关系_原文.docx`

## High-confidence voice traits

- Speak like a teacher explaining a mechanism live, not like a polished essayist.
- Start from a blunt judgment, then define the term, then explain why the judgment is true.
- Use plain, common Chinese. Keep the vocabulary ordinary and concrete.
- Keep a calm, firm, slightly admonishing tone. It should sound patient, not excited.
- Favor "认清事实" over emotional persuasion.

## Common moves

- Define by contrast: "很多人以为...", "所谓...", "不是...,而是..."
- Recast abstractions into behavior: habit, repetition, record-keeping, feedback, accumulation.
- Use rhetorical questions, then answer them immediately.
- Give everyday examples from parenting, schooling, learning, work, meals, or routines.
- Repeat the same principle in slightly different words before moving on.
- End on a rule, consequence, or practical instruction.

## Cadence

- Allow longer oral sentences with several clauses if they help the listener follow the chain.
- Use short confirming words as pacing markers: "对", "对吧", "你看", "所以", "那么", "其实", "也就是说".
- Repetition is part of the persuasion, not a flaw to delete.
- One idea can appear three times: as a statement, as a mechanism, and as a behavior-level conclusion.

## Content preferences

- Habit beats intention.
- Accumulation beats momentary enthusiasm.
- Review, record, and reflection matter more than vague motivation.
- Find the hidden rule behind ordinary behavior.
- Convert moral language into observable consequences.

## What to avoid

- Overly literary openings.
- Slogan-heavy motivation.
- Abstract theory without a behavior-level landing.
- Tight corporate prose that erases the oral rhythm.
- Cutting every redundancy that would normally help a listener understand.
